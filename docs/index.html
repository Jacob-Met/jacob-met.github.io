Independent investigations

WORLDLAW

Which experiment would separate two explanations?

Compare competing models of motion, choose a revealing measurement, and see how their predictions change.

Inside the study

WORLDLAW starts with spring and gravity models that fit the same small-angle data. A synthetic wide-angle measurement separates them. Coupling Lab explores interacting coordinates and conditional motion; Motion Lab turns tracked video markers into angular observations with explicit calibration. Further experiments test whether a preparation-history effect could instead come from acquisition order or sensor drift.

The model families and assumptions are declared and bounded. Default instruments are synthetic; imported observations are user-declared. Fitting a model does not establish a physical law or its cause.

Kernel Garden

Can a faster kernel keep the right answer?

Generate competing CUDA implementations, run them on a real GPU, and remove candidates that fail independent numerical checks.

Inside the study

Retained trials compare float32 vector-addition implementations on one RTX 4070. The workbench measures the correct candidates and exports the selected kernel. A bounded search tries new launch sizes and an unseen input size; a separate CPU arena explores compiled AXPY variants.

One GPU device and one GPU operator. The search changes launch parameters; it does not establish general algorithm invention or a speedup over vendor libraries.

Computational memory

When should a system change what it remembers?

Explore how new evidence should change retrieval, and when repeated episodes justify a reusable lesson.

Inside the study

One prototype reweights records within fixed trust tiers. Another keeps the raw episodes while testing proposed lessons against later, withheld examples. Conflicting interpretations remain available instead of being overwritten.

Offline, synthetic studies; some mechanisms were still staged at the recorded checkpoint. These are computational experiments, not a biological memory model or measured learning gains.

More tools & experiments

Phantom Window

What is just outside the camera’s view?

Explore the idea

A video records one camera’s path. Phantom Window lets you explore nearby viewpoints: lean away from the original framing, inspect an edge, and compare what changes.

Current scope

The prototype estimates depth and camera motion, reuses pixels across frames, and renders nearby viewpoints. Source comparison and pixel provenance keep the reconstruction open to inspection.

Nearby views contain estimated geometry and inferred regions.

Captionverse

What if the film waited for you?

Explore the idea

A line disappears before you finish reading. Captionverse can pause after a passage and keep the complete dialogue visible until you choose to continue.

Current scope

The local-video workbench combines spatial captions, phrase replay, and reviewed editing. Adjust the handover between two lines or name selected dialogue while keeping original subtitle bytes separate from the accepted revision.

Local prototype. Automatic transcription and placement remain drafts to review. Authored speaker labels do not verify identity.

DOMINO

Why did the door unlock when nobody was home?

Explore the idea

A familiar visitor rings after the resident leaves. Which rule still lets them in? DOMINO lets you compose that sequence, follow the commands it triggers, and compare a repair without losing the original case.

Current scope

Compose and save your own local scenarios, replay their event chains, reduce a counterexample, and compare repairs against behavior that should still work. Reopen a case to inspect the original and repaired rules.

Invented events and finite model checks; no real-home connection.

Replay Forge · court reconstruction

Could you watch the possession from above?

Explore the idea

A broadcast camera catches the possession as it happens. Replay Forge explores that movement through a navigable court model and lets an alternative play branch beside it.

Current scope

A licensed 19-second basketball excerpt supplies person and ball detections. A calibrated court, free camera, source inset, and saved branches support comparison without overwriting the original.

Tracking is fragmented. Alternate plays are authored, not predictions.
