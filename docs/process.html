AI / authorship / accountability

Judge the work. Understand the process.

I do not think a tool label tells you, by itself, whether something is good.

AI expands what I can attempt. It does not remove my responsibility for what I release.

I use AI in research, software, writing, and design. I don't claim that every line of code or prose was typed by me, or that every image was hand-drawn. My part includes choosing the questions, directing the work, inspecting results, making editorial and engineering decisions, and deciding what is ready to stand behind.

That does not make the outputs good by definition. More output is not the same as better work. A test count is not universal correctness. A generated reader simulation is not a human audience. An attractive render is not a finished physical object.

Different work needs different evidence.

Science

Methods, provenance, uncertainty, and the distinction between observations and interpretations matter. Fictional or generated data cannot be presented as observed research data. This site does not publish confidential participant records or claim clinical validation.

Software

Inspect the source, run the supported examples, and examine the tests and limitations. A demonstrated feature has a scope. AI-assisted implementation needs review just as other implementation does.

Fiction and art

Read and look closely. Character, language, atmosphere, coherence, and the experience of the work matter. Process transparency is not a substitute for those qualities, nor does a tool label settle them.

Making

A concept, model, fabrication attempt, fitted object, and finished costume are different stages. Photographs, iteration, and material constraints tell a story that a concept render alone cannot.

Credit should be specific.

Collaborators, institutions, upstream software, references, and original creators deserve accurate attribution. Using AI is not a claim to have done everyone else's work, and directing a project is not the same as inventing every component.

A record, not a victory lap.

I want the case for this way of working to come from a growing body of substantive work and visible care. This site is part of making that record inspectable, including the unfinished parts and the things still to learn.
